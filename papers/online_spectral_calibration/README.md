# Spectral Calibration Without a Split

*Learning covariance eigenvalue corrections one observation at a time.* Peter Cotton.

**Status:** draft note, not submitted.

Cross-validated covariance cleaning has to partition a sample to learn what variance belongs to an
empirical eigenvector. A stream does not: the basis carried forward from past data is already out of
sample with respect to the next observation, so the arrow of time supplies the split for free and
every observation labels every eigenvalue at once. The note gives the conditional identity, the
exact Frobenius decomposition that makes squared projections the right target, a one-pass estimator
costing O(p²) per observation, and the result we did not expect — the learned correction is beaten,
wherever dependence drifts, by putting the *analytical* map on a rolling window.
